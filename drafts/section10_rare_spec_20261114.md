Rows that do not meet it are reported too. (c) Method: BCa (bias-corrected and accelerated; Efron, JASA 82:171-185, 1987) on the registered target bootstrap. The replicates are the registered ones: the same 10,000 draws of targets from seed 2026 over the same sorted common targets, and the same pooled rates (the sum of the flips over the units of the drawn targets divided by their number). The percentile interval is therefore recomputed first, and it must equal the registered one within 1e-9; a row that does not is marked "not reproduced" and is not re-read. Bias correction z0 = Phi^-1(p), p = (number of replicates below the estimate + half the number equal to it) / 10,000; acceleration a from the jackknife over targets (leave one target out); limits at the replicate percentiles Phi(z0 + (z0 + z)/(1 - a(z0 + z))) for z = -1.96 and 1.96, with numpy's linear interpolation, as the registered intervals. If every replicate equals the estimate (no event on any term), the interval is the estimate and the row says so. If p is 0 or 1, BCa is undefined and the row says so. Beside it, for single rates only, the two-sided 95% Clopper-Pearson upper limit on the fraction of targets with at least one event. It bounds the per-prediction rate from above whatever the correlation within a target, so it is conservative; it is reported, not read. (d) Why BCa and not an exact target-level method: BCa keeps the registered estimand, the clustering by target and the registered draws, so a change of reading comes from how the limits are taken and from nothing else. It corrects the bias and skewness of the replicate distribution, which are largest when events are few (a rate with a handful of events is discrete, right-skewed and bounded at 0), where the percentile interval is only first-order accurate. An exact target-level method answers a different question. It bounds the fraction of targets with an event, not the per-prediction rate, and it gives no interval on the same scale for the paired differences HW minus RC and D_F. At 60 targets its upper limit with no event is 5.96 points, above follow-up 14's 5-point threshold, so the "agree" rule could not be met by any data. Limits of BCa, stated before any value: it is undefined or degenerate without events, and with few events in few targets (HW(G,C) of the main study has 6 targets) the jackknife acceleration is coarse. (e) Reading rule: each family's registered rule applied to the BCa interval. Beyond recompilation if the lower limit is above 0 (F1 to F4); agree if the upper limit is below 5.0 points (F6); reduced if the upper limit is below 0 (F5); F7 none. A reading "changes" if the BCa reading differs from the registered reading. The report gives the number of readings that change and lists them. The paper may say how many would change. It does not replace a registered reading. No correction for multiple comparisons.
